Life-course research has studied turning points — events that durably redirect a trajectory — since at least Elder's work in the 1980s, and has developed a range of ways to identify them. These projects look for turning points at scale in longitudinal records: which diagnoses redirect a patient's disease trajectory in primary care, and which events in early adulthood shift a life trajectory, for whom.

Research on gender in higher education has tracked representation and attrition since the 1970s and documented the role of departmental climate. These papers add large-scale evidence from the employment records of nearly every professor at US universities, paired with survey and interview data: women leave faculty positions at higher rates than men at every career stage, tenure included, and faculty who leave more often cite their workplace than anything pulling them elsewhere.

Dyslexia affects roughly one in five people in the US, and benefits from early detection, but screening tests are applied inconsistently and classroom teachers are not generally trained to administer them. These two workshop papers contributed handwriting datasets from children with and without dyslexia, a computer-vision classifier intended to run alongside existing screening, and a follow-up examining whether such a system should be used at all.
